C. TKA Reading Strategy Guide

General Strategy Flow: 1. READ → 2. FIND → 3. THINK → 4. ANSWER

Step 1 • READ

Read the question carefully and understand what information it asks for.

Step 2 • FIND

Scan the reading passage using keywords to locate the relevant paragraph or sentence.

Step 3 • THINK

Analyze the clues and check for supporting evidence in the text—not personal guesses.

Step 4 • ANSWER

Choose or formulate the most accurate answer backed by textual evidence.

Three Core TKA Reading Comprehension Skills

Skill 1

Textual Understanding

Finding information stated directly (explicitly) in the passage.

Formula: Spot the keyword → Locate the information → Pick the answer.

Skill 2

Inferential Understanding

Drawing logical conclusions from explicit and implicit clues in the text.

Formula: Look for clues → Link the information → Draw a conclusion.

Skill 3

Evaluating Information Based on Evidence

Judging whether a statement is supported by evidence from the text.

Formula: Read the statement → Look back at the text → Find the evidence → Make a decision.

A. What Is an Analytical Exposition?

Definition: An analytical exposition is a text that presents the writer’s argument about a topic in order to persuade the reader that something is the case or that something should be done.

C. Interactive Model Reading Text

Highlight Language Features & Vocabulary:

SCHOOLS SHOULD REDUCE SINGLE-USE PLASTIC

Single-use plastic has become a part of daily life in many schools. Students buy snacks in plastic wrappers and drink from plastic bottles or cups every day. In my opinion, schools should reduce the use of single-use plastic because it harms the environment and creates unnecessary waste.

Firstly, single-use plastic needs hundreds of years to decompose. Most of it is thrown away after only a few minutes of use, so the waste piles up quickly in landfills and rivers. As a result, the soil and water become polluted, and marine life is put in danger.

Secondly, reducing plastic can build good habits among students. When schools provide refill stations and encourage students to bring reusable bottles, students learn to be responsible for their environment. In addition, they can save money because they do not have to buy bottled drinks every day.

In conclusion, schools should reduce single-use plastic. By choosing reusable items, students and teachers can keep the school clean and protect the environment for future generations.

Learning Summary, Knowledge Check & Reflection

Consolidate what you learned about analytical exposition and reading strategies, verify your mastery with a quick check, and complete your student reflection.

1. Communicative Purpose

Analytical exposition presents the writer’s arguments to persuade readers that something is the case or should be done.

2. Generic Structure

Thesis (position) → Arguments (reasons + evidence) → Reiteration (restatement & conclusion).

3. Language Features

Simple Present Tense, Modals (should, must, can), Connectives (firstly, therefore), and Evaluative Words.

4. Evidence-Based TKA Reading

Use READ → FIND → THINK → ANSWER. Always justify explicit, inferential, and evaluative answers with textual evidence.

CITIES SHOULD BAN FREE SINGLE-USE PLASTIC BAGS

Every day, millions of single-use plastic bags are handed out for free in supermarkets and traditional markets. Although convenient for carrying groceries for twenty minutes, these lightweight bags frequently escape open bins and clog urban drainage systems. Consequently, cities should prohibit free single-use plastic bags to prevent flooding and long-term microplastic contamination.

Firstly, blocked drains are a major cause of seasonal street flooding in densely populated towns. When thin plastic bags accumulate inside stormwater pipes, rainwater cannot flow smoothly, causing damage to homes and public roads. Secondly, plastic bags break down into tiny microplastics rather than disappearing completely, which eventually enter local waterways and food chains.

In conclusion, banning free single-use plastic bags encourages shoppers to carry durable cloth totes and forces retailers to adopt sustainable packaging. Clear municipal policies protect both city infrastructure and public health.
