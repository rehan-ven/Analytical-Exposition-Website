Part C · Explicit Strategy Instruction

The Four-Step Reading Strategy

1. READ

Read the question carefully and identify exactly what information it asks for.

2. FIND

Scan the passage for keywords and locate the relevant sentence or paragraph.

3. THINK

Analyze the clues, connect ideas, and verify supporting evidence inside the text.

4. ANSWER

Select or write the clearest answer backed directly by textual evidence.

Three TKA Reading Comprehension Skills

1. Textual Understanding

Finding information stated directly (explicitly) in the passage.

Spot keyword → Locate info → Pick answer
2. Inferential Understanding

Drawing a logical conclusion from clues and ideas in the passage.

Look for clues → Link ideas → Conclude
3. Evaluating with Evidence

Judging whether a claim is supported by facts in the text, not personal opinion.

Read claim → Check text → Find proof

Part A · Genre Knowledge

What Is an Analytical Exposition?

An analytical exposition is a text that presents the writer's argument about a topic in order to persuade the reader that something is the case or that something should be done.

Model Text · Original Unaltered Passage
SCHOOLS SHOULD REDUCE SINGLE-USE PLASTIC

Single-use plastic has become a part of daily life in many schools. Students buy snacks in plastic wrappers and drink from plastic bottles or cups every day. In my opinion, schools should reduce the use of single-use plastic because it harms the environment and creates unnecessary waste.

Firstly, single-use plastic needs hundreds of years to decompose. Most of it is thrown away after only a few minutes of use, so the waste piles up quickly in landfills and rivers. As a result, the soil and water become polluted, and marine life is put in danger.

Secondly, reducing plastic can build good habits among students. When schools provide refill stations and encourage students to bring reusable bottles, students learn to be responsible for their environment. In addition, they can save money because they do not have to buy bottled drinks every day.

In conclusion, schools should reduce single-use plastic. By choosing reusable items, students and teachers can keep the school clean and protect the environment for future generations.

Language Features Reference
  • Simple Present Tense: States facts & general truths (Plastic waste harms marine life.)
  • Modals (should, must, can): Expresses recommendations & necessity (Schools should reduce single-use plastic.)
  • Connectives: Links arguments logically (Firstly, secondly, in addition, therefore, in conclusion)
  • Evaluative Words: Signals the writer's attitude (harmful, wasteful, important, necessary, responsible)

Schools should provide separate bins for organic waste, paper, and plastic. Firstly, sorting waste makes recycling much easier. When paper and plastic are collected separately, they can be reused and turned into new products instead of ending up in the landfill.

1. Purpose

Persuasive Arguments

An analytical exposition presents the writer's arguments to persuade readers that an issue is important or that an action should be taken.

2. Generic Structure

Thesis → Arguments → Reiteration

Opens with the writer's position (Thesis), supports it with reasons and evidence (Arguments), and restates the claim (Reiteration).

3. Language Features

Grammar & Vocabulary

Uses Simple Present Tense, modals (should, must, can), logical connectives (firstly, therefore), and evaluative words (harmful, responsible).

4. Reading Proof

Evidence-Based Answers

Strong readers find explicit facts, make inferences from clues, and always support answers with exact evidence from the passage.

Support for Learners · Reading Strategy Card

READ → FIND → THINK → ANSWER

Step 1 · READ

Underline what the question asks (e.g., how long, what, why, which statement).

Step 2 · FIND

Match the keyword in the question to the matching sentence in the paragraph.

Step 3 · THINK

For direct facts, copy the detail. For inference, connect two clues together.

Step 4 · ANSWER

Check your choice against the exact words in the text: "My answer is … because the text says …"

Quick Hints for Each Question Type

  • Textual Understanding: Look for exact time, object, or action words (Spot keyword → Locate info → Pick answer).
  • Inferential Understanding: Ask yourself: "Why did the writer mention this detail?" (Look for clues → Link ideas → Draw conclusion).
  • Evaluating with Evidence: Eliminate options that contradict the text or add facts not mentioned in the passage.

Optional Enrichment Extension · For Scores 76 and Above

Advanced Passage: Banning Single-Use Plastic Utensils in School Cafeterias

SCHOOL CAFETERIAS SHOULD PHASE OUT PLASTIC UTENSILS

Every lunch period, school cafeterias distribute hundreds of disposable plastic forks, spoons, and straws that are discarded within twenty minutes. Although single-use utensils appear convenient, schools should phase them out because their long-term environmental cost far outweighs their brief convenience.

First, lightweight plastic utensils rarely get recycled. Because they are small and often contaminated with food, sorting machines cannot process them efficiently, sending them straight to landfills or waterways where they fragment into microplastics. Second, transitioning to washable metal cutlery or bamboo alternatives encourages mindful consumption. When schools invest in durable dishware and washing stations, students witness sustainability in daily practice rather than only reading about it in textbooks.

In conclusion, phasing out disposable cafeteria plastics is both practical and educational. By replacing throwaway utensils with durable options, schools lower waste volumes and model responsible citizenship.
